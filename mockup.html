Chapter Three

The Beginning of Knowledge

The Reverend Mother Gaius Helen Mohiam came down from her chambers, walking slowly through the cool stone corridors of Castle Caladan, where centuries of Atreides ancestors had walked before her. She paused at the arched window overlooking the night-dark sea. Far below, the waves moved with the unhurried patience of something that knew it would outlast every kingdom.

Paul lay awake in his chamber. He had felt the test before it began — not as memory, but as something older, a knowing that came from somewhere beneath thought. His mother had warned him, in her oblique Bene Gesserit way, that the day would come when he would be measured against an ancient standard. The standard, he understood now, was not knowledge or skill. It was the willingness to remain still while pain crawled the surface of the mind.

He closed his hand into a fist, then opened it. The chamber smelled faintly of melange — the spice that drifted through every corridor of Caladan now that the Imperial decree had been read aloud in the great hall. Arrakis. The word still felt strange in his mouth, as if it belonged to a language he had not yet learned.
